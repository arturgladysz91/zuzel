
# Study #217 continuation · H36-C

The starting controller audit was published at
`7551d6ce7948b6c53d45091409bb39a30cd51181` before this work.
H36-C continues the same study. It uses the H35-S dynamics and
keeps the original Drive and steering-clamp diagnostics available.
The qualification target here is a continuous balanced MODEL
bend on the 97.389 m reference section with eight metres of runout.

## Spatial vehicle and swept clearance

The #216 screen subtracted another 1.35 m after measuring an already
sized planar motorcycle footprint. H36 replaces that second blanket
allowance with separate spatial capsules for the frame, engine, fork,
bars, footrests, wheels, torso, head, arms, legs and boots. Chassis
capsules use the frame COM and attitude. Rider capsules use the rider
COM and position relative to the frame. The left shoe follows the
H35 leg actuator and the hands span the moving bars. Each wheel
uses twelve oriented ring segments, with the chord sagitta added
to the projected tube radius. This preserves a conservative
enclosure of the wheel between sampled angles.

The 2025 FIM specifications bound 250/500 cc track-racing handlebar
width to 0.70–0.90 m, the right footrest to 0.32 m from the
centreline, and the speedway tyre widths to 0.08 m front and
0.10 m rear. The occupancy model uses a 0.80 m bar and 0.28 m
right footrest. Its 0.05 m wheel section radius conservatively
covers both tyres. The H35 left support location remains as a
small rigid ledge in this model. Engine and rider dimensions are
provisional. The compact/large variants use 0.095/0.135 m engine
radius, −0.12/−0.18 m engine centre height relative to the frame
origin, bar widths 0.70/0.90 m and rider radii scaled by 0.90/1.10.
The extreme variants are sensitivity envelopes. [FIM Track Racing
Technical Regulations, sections 33, 39 and 49](https://www.fim-moto.com/fileadmin/user_upload/Documents/2025/2025_V2_TRACK_RACING_Technical_Regulations_10.05.2025.pdf?t=1747928363)

The same shaped-track boundary engine measures the new projected
capsules, including the existing 0.7 mm edge-chord allowance and
0.1 mm numerical allowance. The search can use a certified enclosing
disk in wide-open space and resolves individual capsules near the
wall. Qualification always replays with individual capsules.
For an accepted integration interval, the transport bound sums
COM displacement, rotation times the maximum arm, rider-relative
velocity, leg-extension speed and bar or wheel steering speed.
The lower bound is the smaller endpoint clearance minus half the
whole-interval transport bound. The 0.50 m threshold applies
to that lower bound. For ground protection, only the vertical
component of COM travel contributes. The wheel and boot still
use the original contact laws.

The four old chassis points represent the lower corners of a
rectangular envelope. H36 checks contact against the spatial
hard parts and also checks the swept vertical margin. The
60° overall orientation domain, tyre compression envelope,
ground contacts, tyre force budgets and actuator limits stay
in effect. The archive audit replays each recorded #216 clearance
or chassis rejection to its original frontier, then tests the
same states with the new geometry. A reclassified rejection
certifies that archived prefix under the new envelope. The
course beyond the original frontier is outside that test.

## Dynamically reached states and planning

The common seed is a measured 40 km/h straight equilibrium in the
model. It is placed 51 m before the entry, then integrated to the
approach checkpoint 33 m before entry. Spatial arc following
uses lookahead to the planned line on the physical course.
Roll demand comes from the velocity and path curvature. An
inverse calculation brackets the desired roll rate by integrating
the possible steering commands through the real angle servo after
the pending 50 ms command delay. The outer loop commands Drive
and body position through the physical actuators. No state field
is overwritten during an approach, corner, exit or verification.

The offline search tests whole routes with varied line, apex
offset, roll gains, rider position and leg demand. The budget is
52 proposals per speed. The previous successful run supplies its
command tape, line and dynamically generated checkpoint. Matching
variants replay the earlier prefix and replan the rest, so a later
candidate can change its entry before the previously reached bend.
The selected maneuver is re-executed from the original seed with
the recorded command tape and exact swept checks. An independent
collar of post-runout continuation is checked from the actual
terminal state and its pending commands.

The result categories are straight/circle mechanics, balanced
MODEL bend, measured slide intervals, official strict sliding
qualification and sporting qualification. The duration of a
measured slide is based on actual sideslip and rear contact.
That measurement is recorded separately from the official
qualification rules.

## Geometry findings and the 40 km/h baseline

| Archived #216 rejection | Replayed | H36 result |
|---|---:|---|
| Swept track clearance | 206 | All 206 complete archived prefixes passed the 0.50 m projected sweep and hard-part ground checks. Their certified minimum track clearances ranged from 0.940 to 1.906 m. |
| Chassis-ground witness | 264 | 261 rejected endpoints had positive nominal hard-part clearance. Three still touched the ground with the spatial geometry. The compact and large envelopes retained one and six contacts, respectively. |

The archived prefixes contributed 121,276 accepted mechanical
steps. An additional 11,202 dense-pose evaluations found no
optimistic lower bound. For a statically supported left lean,
the old witness first rejected the 1° grid at 51°. The nominal
hard parts still cleared the ground by 16.8 mm at that
angle and first reached the ground on the grid at 54°. These
results demonstrate that the old envelope rejected part of the
plausible range. The 54° contact is specific to this geometry
and static setup.

At 40 km/h, the straight equilibrium had a 2 s roll drift below
0.000001 rad and tyre utilization 0.050. The steady circle ran
at 42.927 m radius for 2 s. Its measured lateral acceleration
was 2.876 m/s², its roll was −0.295 rad, and peak axle
utilization at the measured equilibrium was 0.191. The
actual steering angle was 0.0307 rad. The measured local
front-moment bands were 21.78 N·m for yaw and 16.76 N·m for
roll at the integration sample. The circle equilibrium
therefore supports tracking this radius at the first speed.

A write failure when appending the 50 km/h journal left the
first 50 km/h search receipts but omitted the stage timing
receipt. The journal now replaces its last complete file
atomically. The exact 50 km/h proposals were rerun from the
archived 40 km/h checkpoint to produce the recorded timing
and to continue the curriculum.

The base 80 km/h search completed zero of 52 candidates. Forty-four
stopped at a forecast of chassis contact and five reached a swept
chassis ground limit. The furthest candidate stopped 20.89 m past
entry. The wider line and the pace schedule completed 11 of 52
candidates, with a highest entry of 77.52 km/h. The best near-band
pace replay entered at 77.50 km/h, reached 67.18 km/h at midpoint
and exited at 67.30 km/h. The four approach-only trims paired two of
these successful settings with two corrections of the measured
shortfall. The one in-band success used a commanded correction of
2.484 km/h and received its speed solely from the drivetrain.
At runout its roll was −0.0410 rad and yaw rate was 0.00093 rad/s.
The nominal minimum swept hard-part ground margin during the run
was 0.264 mm, so the 79.68 km/h result is sensitive to the
provisional chassis dimensions. The 70 km/h selected replay
retained 12.0 mm of ground margin.

The base 90 km/h search had 40 forecast chassis refusals, six
forecast orientation refusals, four spatial track-clearance refusals
and two actual swept ground-margin refusals. One candidate reached an
88.03 km/h entry. The deepest candidate stopped at 18.70 m past
entry with −0.918 rad roll, on the outside of its intended line.
This separates the dominant forecast refusal from the actual
track-clearance limit of the particular deepest candidate.

The 48 additional candidates kept the wide approach, advanced
the entry pace target and shed planned pace before the midpoint.
All 48 reached a predicted chassis-ground limit. The selected
candidate's exact replay entered at 91.16 km/h with −0.232 rad
roll and stopped 13.14 m into the bend at a real swept chassis
limit. Its last accepted sample had 0.127 mm of ground reserve,
0.922 m of certified track clearance and −0.928 rad roll. It
still carried 86.51 km/h horizontally. Peak tyre utilization was
0.810 and peak active rider force was 422 N. This run was
limited by the achieved roll, line and pace preparation under
the chassis envelope. The tests leave other physically reachable
90 km/h approaches open.

| 120 wheels gate | 3.880 → 5.307 m | 36.06 → 20.32 m/s² | 731 N / 12.44 N·m / 9.36 N·m |

The old predictor logged 24, 24 and 18 swept refusals in these
three representative cases. With the spatial screen and the
new candidates, each exact abstention state verified a continuous
0.24 s horizon. Candidate selection and the former clearance
screen therefore matter. The 1.05 s probes still failed when the
roll forecast ran out of clearance from the ground, including
the alternative wider, slower candidate. Rewinding approximately
0.38 s and allowing up to 1.45 s exposed the same forecast failure.
The positive last accepted ground margins distinguish these forecasts
from observed chassis strikes.

The force snapshots show a deficit against the demand of the imminent
radius and little available front roll or yaw moment on these paths.
The shared muscle force reached 700 N in the snapshots. Power was
4.8, 158 and 26.9 W, below the 350 W budget. The snapshots and
limited recourse establish neither irrecoverability nor a universal
tyre-force deficit for another line, approach or speed history.
Short horizons can accept a trajectory that later runs out of
roll authority. Terminal choice and late preparation were varied
through earlier and longer probes, with no independently causal
verdict on either in these three archived runs. No probe failed
with numerical certification as its first reported cause.

## Matched left-foot contact

The 40 and 50 km/h pairs both completed with the foot deployed and
parked. Active contact peaked at 108.3 and 194.7 N, respectively.
Exit speed changed by −0.007 and −0.017 km/h. At 60 km/h both
reached the exit and failed the terminal balance gate. In the
70 km/h pair the parked run passed, while the deployed run
failed terminal balance with a measured peak boot load of 428.3 N.
The 80 and 90 km/h base pairs both stopped before qualification
and recorded no boot reaction. The paired evidence shows genuine
external support in some runs and no improvement in qualification
or exit speed in the successful pairs tested.

## Answers to the required questions

1. **Can the H35-S dynamics complete a full bend at 40–60 km/h?**
   Yes, with H36's physical clearance geometry and controller.
   Continuous exact replays pass all three bands, traversing the
   entire 97.389 m reference bend and at least 8 m beyond it.
2. **What prevents completion in that range?** There is no general
   barrier in that range. Several candidates fail, including the
   matched 60 km/h foot pair at terminal balance. The successful
   60 km/h replay shows the dynamics support a different choice of
   line, controls and balance recovery.
3. **How many clearance refusals were conservative?** All 206
   archived track-clearance refusals passed for their historical
   prefixes under the new screen. In a separate chassis set,
   261 of 264 rejected endpoints had nominal hard-part clearance.
4. **Does a 20–40 m approach solve preparation?** The moving
   checkpoint 33 m before entry sufficed for all qualified bands.
   The motorcycle arrived at that checkpoint under integration from
   51 m out. The tested 90 km/h approaches still failed, so
   distance alone cannot explain or solve preparation there.
5. **Highest dynamically reached entry followed by a complete
   bend?** 79.68 km/h, with 54.81 km/h at midpoint, 48.24 km/h
   minimum and 48.29 km/h at exit. Its 0.264 mm minimum chassis
   margin makes this a nominal-model result.
6. **Can the rider sustain a slide and recover balance?** Yes,
   under the measured diagnostic definition of at least 6°
   vehicle sideslip with more than 20 N rear normal load for at least
   0.12 s. The selected 70 and 80 km/h replays held consecutive
   intervals of 0.426 and 0.612 s and passed the runout balance
   gate. The 80 km/h replay recorded 0.832 s total above the
   sideslip and load thresholds. Official strict sliding qualification
   remains open.
7. **Full Drive in a successful complete bend?** The separate
   40 km/h entry witness held active Drive = 1 for 1.036 s
   inside the bend, before the nominal exit. It exited at
   60.91 km/h. The selected 70 and 80 km/h slide witnesses
   recorded zero full Drive in the bend. Sustained slide and
   full Drive were demonstrated on different passes.
   In this model Drive scales the engine's normalized throttle input
   before impairment, engine-map, loss and drivetrain calculations.
8. **Does left-foot contact improve the maneuver?** The matched
   results establish no improvement. Both settings passed at 40
   and 50, both failed terminal balance at 60, and at 70 only the
   parked-foot setting passed the terminal balance gate.
9. **What remains incomplete?** A complete qualified 90 km/h
   entry, the 100–120 km/h progression gated behind it, strict
   sliding qualification, sporting and native qualification, a
   race-pace proof with sustained full Drive through the slide,
   and any FAST50 certification. The 90 km/h result identifies
   where the tested controller runs out of balance and chassis
   clearance. It leaves the globally reachable envelope open.

The straight and circle pass the mechanical baseline. The full
bends through the 80 km/h band pass the separate balanced MODEL
gate. The diagnostic slide intervals are measured outcomes.
Official strict, sporting and native results remain unqualified.
Zero native worlds were constructed. The H25 default and official
qualification profile remain at the audit baseline.
